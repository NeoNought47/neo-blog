## 1. White Balance 白平衡

让照片里的白色看起来是白的，避免整体偏黄或偏蓝。

- **White balance presets**：告诉相机现在是什么光（日光、阴天、阴影、白炽灯、荧光灯等）
- 根据你看到的光源选对应的预设，拿不准就用 Auto

相机设置：`M 模式 > 按 Q > 找到 White Balance`

## 2. ISO

感光度：相机对光的敏感程度（严格说是对传感器信号的放大）。

- 光线不够时调高 ISO，让相机更「敏感」
- **ISO 越高，噪点越多**
- 推荐范围 100 ~ 800，尽量不超过 1600

相机设置：`M 模式 > 机身顶部 ISO 按钮 > 按住并转拨轮`

### 老师给的 ISO 速查表

| 场景 | 建议 ISO |
|---|---|
| 室外，晴天 | 100 ~ 400 |
| 室外，阴天或黄昏 | 400 ~ 800 |
| 室内，有窗户自然光 | 200 ~ 400 |
| 室内，主要靠台灯或室内灯 | 400 ~ 800 |
| 室外夜晚 | 800 ~ 1600 |

不要超过 ISO 1600，否则会出现明显的数字噪点 / 颗粒（digital noise / grain）。

如果 ISO 已经调到 800，画面还是太暗，就去调 **光圈** 和 / 或 **快门速度**，让更多光进入相机。

## 3. Shutter Speed 快门速度

快门打开多久，也就是曝光时长（1/15、1/400 等）。

相机设置：`按 Q > 分数形式的那个数字`

- 决定动态怎么被记录：**快门慢会拍出动感模糊，快门快会把动作冻结**
- 手持时慢于 1/30 很容易出现模糊
- 手持建议 1/60 或更快，避免手抖。经验法则：快门不要慢于 1/焦距（比如 50mm 镜头用 1/50 以上）
- 想用慢快门拍拖影，上三脚架

## 4. Aperture 光圈

镜头开口的大小，控制进光量，同时也决定景深。

相机设置：`按 Q > F 开头的数字就是光圈`

- **数字越小，光圈越大**。f/1.8 = 光圈大：进光多、景深浅、背景更容易虚化
- **数字越大，光圈越小**。f/8 = 光圈小：进光少、景深深、前后都更容易清晰
- f/2.8、f/4 这类大光圈，清晰的范围比较窄

## 曝光调整的顺序

照片太暗时，**先调光圈和快门速度，最后才动 ISO**。ISO 是代价最大的那个（噪点）。

## Lenses 镜头

镜头影响：

1. **视角**（焦距）：画面能装进多少场景
2. **景深与对焦**：能虚化到什么程度、最近能对多近
3. **曝光**：最大光圈决定了它在暗处能进多少光

## Light Meter 测光表

相机内置，用来判断 ISO、光圈、快门的组合够不够亮。

- 半按快门，取景器里的指针会移动
- **0**：曝光正常
- **正数**：偏亮，过曝
- **负数**：偏暗，欠曝

## 1. White Balance

Makes white look white, so the photo does not come out too yellow or too blue.

- **White balance presets** tell the camera what kind of light you are in (daylight, cloudy, shade, tungsten, fluorescent and so on)
- Pick the preset that matches the light you see. When unsure, use Auto

On the camera: `M mode > press Q > find White Balance`

## 2. ISO

How sensitive the camera is to light (strictly speaking, how much the sensor's signal is amplified).

- Raise ISO when there is not much light, to make the camera more "sensitive"
- **Higher ISO means more noise**
- Recommended range is 100 to 800. Try not to go above 1600

On the camera: `M mode > ISO button on top > hold it and turn the dial`

### ISO cheat sheet from the instructor

| Situation | Suggested ISO |
|---|---|
| Outdoors, sunny | 100 to 400 |
| Outdoors, overcast or at dusk | 400 to 800 |
| Indoors, with natural light from a window | 200 to 400 |
| Indoors, lit mainly by lamps or room lights | 400 to 800 |
| Outdoors at night | 800 to 1600 |

Do not go above ISO 1600, or you will see obvious digital noise and grain.

If you are already at ISO 800 and still need more light for a balanced exposure, let more light in by adjusting the **aperture** and/or the **shutter speed**.

## 3. Shutter Speed

How long the shutter stays open, in other words the length of the exposure (1/15, 1/400 and so on).

On the camera: `press Q > the number shown as a fraction`

- It decides how motion is rendered: **a slow shutter captures motion blur, a fast one freezes the action**
- Handheld, anything slower than 1/30 blurs easily
- Handheld, use 1/60 or faster to avoid camera shake. Rule of thumb: no slower than 1 over the focal length (with a 50mm lens, 1/50 or faster)
- For deliberate motion trails with a slow shutter, use a tripod

## 4. Aperture

The size of the lens opening. It controls how much light comes in, and it also sets the depth of field.

On the camera: `press Q > the number starting with F is the aperture`

- **Smaller number, wider aperture.** f/1.8 is wide: more light, shallow depth of field, an easily blurred background
- **Larger number, narrower aperture.** f/8 is narrow: less light, deeper depth of field, more of the scene sharp from front to back
- Wide apertures like f/2.8 or f/4 keep only a narrow range in focus

## Order of adjustment

If the photo is too dark, **adjust aperture and shutter speed first, and touch ISO last**. ISO is the one with the biggest cost: noise.

## Lenses

A lens affects:

1. **Angle of view** (focal length): how much of the scene fits in the frame
2. **Depth of field and focus**: how much blur you can get and how close you can focus
3. **Exposure**: the maximum aperture decides how much light it can gather in the dark

## Light Meter

Built into the camera, it tells you whether your combination of ISO, aperture and shutter speed is bright enough.

- Half-press the shutter and the needle in the viewfinder moves
- **0**: correct exposure
- **Positive**: too bright, overexposed
- **Negative**: too dark, underexposed
